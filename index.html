PROSPECTIVE REAL-WORLD LIVING STUDY

Personal AI OS

Longitudinal data. Traceable context. Real-world evidence.

I am studying whether a conversational Personal AI Operating System can integrate fragmented everyday data over time, preserve provenance and uncertainty, and produce genuinely useful cross-source context without turning ordinary life into a laboratory protocol.

Publication comes first. This site documents the evolving system, methods, failures, selected case studies, and scientific questions. It is not a product landing page and it does not claim clinical efficacy.

Question

THE STUDY

A real system observed under ordinary living conditions.

The study is a prospective longitudinal single-participant observational feasibility and methods study. The participant continues normal work, family life, training, nutrition and recovery routines. No intervention sequence, randomized treatment or study-driven lifestyle change is imposed.

PRIMARY QUESTION

Can a conversational AI-centered system continuously integrate heterogeneous everyday data, preserve context and provenance, and generate traceable, potentially action-relevant information?

FROZEN CORE

Primary hypotheses cover passive multimodal feasibility and whether cross-source fusion adds context unavailable from a single source. Later analyses may refine operational rules, but the original claims remain frozen.

PUBLICATION PRINCIPLE

Negative findings, connector failures, missingness, source transitions and cases with no added value remain admissible results rather than being cleaned away.

CURRENT PUBLICATION FRAMING

The central scientific question has moved beyond technical feasibility alone: among substantive real-world interactions, when does a persistent Personal AI OS provide incremental value beyond the best plausible single-source tool or simple self-tracking summary — and when does it not?

Convenience alone is not counted as substantive incremental value. Wrong, unsupported and no-added-value events belong in the denominator.

SELECTED EVIDENCE

Examples are illustrations, not proof of efficacy.

These public cases show how the system behaves in practice. They are useful for understanding provenance, cross-source reasoning and workflow design, but they are not substitutes for the prospective denominators and predefined analyses used in the study.

WORKFLOW CASE · FOUR CONNECTED SYSTEMS

A weekly report from ten weeks of history.

LiftTrack, freddy, Google Calendar and Airtable were queried in one natural-language request, synthesized into a weekly review, and written back into the durable data store.

Research relevance: demonstrates cross-source retrieval, synthesis and deliberate write-back; it does not by itself establish incremental value or improved outcomes.

LONGITUDINAL CASE

From roughly 52 minutes to sub-39 over 10 km.

A multi-year running history reconstructed from activities, body composition, recovery context, calendar records, race data and earlier goal conversations.

Research relevance: illustrates longitudinal reconstruction across heterogeneous records, while causal attribution remains limited.

DECISION-SUPPORT CASE

“Should I run today?”

Recovery signals, recent strength work, subjective journal context and calendar constraints are combined into one training decision.

Research relevance: candidate cross-source synthesis event. Final study inclusion depends on the frozen prospective event and source-independence rules.

NEGATIVE-ACTION CASE

Sometimes the useful answer is no workout.

The system can recommend doing less when recent load, recovery and upcoming quality work make additional training unattractive.

Research relevance: useful systems should not be evaluated by engagement or activity maximization alone.

DATA-CAPTURE METHODS

Raw Log → daily QC → curated Journal.

Fast text, voice and image capture is separated from slower reconciliation, timestamp checks, quantity preservation, uncertainty labeling and durable storage.

Research relevance: logging friction and data durability are treated as methods questions, not just UX details.

REALITY CHECK

Connector-level “green” status does not prove that every endpoint is current. Mirrored workouts are not independent evidence. Vendor scores are not direct physiological measurements. Image- or language-model estimates remain estimates. Infrastructure changes are part of the observed system and must remain visible in analysis.

WHY THIS MAY MATTER

The methods problem is bigger than another dashboard.

Personal data are already dense, but they are fragmented across vendors, devices and domains. The difficult part is deciding what is current, independent, measured, derived, duplicated, missing or uncertain — and then determining whether combining it actually adds value.

The contribution I am testing is therefore not “LLM + wearable data”. It is a longitudinal workflow for provenance-aware integration, failure logging, transparent uncertainty, durable context and prospective evaluation of incremental value.

A fluent answer is not the outcome. A traceable, nonredundant and genuinely useful synthesis might be.

LIMITATIONS & GOVERNANCE

The hard parts stay visible.

  • Single participant and participant-investigator overlap limit external validity.
  • Self-evaluation creates expectancy and selection bias.
  • Vendor algorithms, APIs, devices and connectors can change during follow-up.
  • Multiple services can mirror the same upstream event and create pseudoreplication.
  • Nutrition, symptom and contextual records may be selective or AI-estimated.
  • Prospective synthesis/value-event capture must include failures and no-added-value events.
  • Formal ethics/privacy determination remains a publication release gate.

SAFETY / PRIVACY / DISCLAIMER

A research project, not medical advice.

  • AI-generated interpretations can be wrong.
  • Health-related outputs are informational and decision-support only, not medical advice.
  • Important decisions should be independently verified.
  • Third-party services and AI/connectors process information under their own architectures and terms.
  • Public examples are deliberately minimized; sensitive raw health, family, calendar and location data are not published by default.
  • The eventual reproducibility package should favor protocol, definitions, code, schemas and synthetic/deidentified examples over unnecessary release of raw personal data.
