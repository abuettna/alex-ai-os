CASE STUDY · WORKOUT REVIEW

Does this workout fit my week?

A Push workout was already planned in LiftTrack. I asked my AI coach to check it against my recent training and the rest of my plans.

LiftTrack · freddy · Google Calendar · Airtable

THE ANSWER IN THIS EXCHANGE

Keep the template.
Moderate the effort.

The coach recommended keeping the planned session while avoiding another progression test. No workout edit was needed or demonstrated.

WHAT WAS CHECKED

The template meets the rest of the week.

The visible activity messages report lookups across four connected sources. The response then discusses the template, previous strength sessions, recovery and upcoming training together.

LiftTrack

The planned Push template and recent workout history: what was intended, and what had already been done.

freddy / Garmin

Recent activity and recovery context, made available through the connected health-data service.

Google Calendar

The surrounding training schedule, including the next running sessions.

Airtable

Subjective journal context that adds information beyond the workout template.

HOW THE ANSWER BECAME SPECIFIC

Keep the plan. Adjust how it is executed.

The response went beyond confirming that a workout existed. This is an editorial summary of its reasoning, with personal details omitted:

  1. Check the schedule. Consider how the planned strength session fits around recent and upcoming endurance training.
  2. Compare the template with actual history. Distinguish the intended session from a fresh attempt to increase loads or repetitions.
  3. Use recovery and journal context. Bring the additional sources into the recommendation instead of judging the template alone.
  4. Return a practical answer. Retain the exercise template and recommend controlled effort rather than forcing progression.

The useful output was a decision about effort within an existing workout.

EVIDENCE & PRIVACY

What this example establishes.

The screenshots document a scheduled workout, a user request, visible connector activity messages and the resulting recommendation. They do not independently verify every retrieved record or establish that the recommendation was optimal.

This is a workflow case study. No completed workout, training benefit or clinical outcome is demonstrated. The excerpt is not a general recommendation to train when unwell or in pain.

Only selected text is published. Exact dates, personal measurements, detailed training records and private journal content are omitted. Full screenshots and underlying records are not included in this case study’s public assets.

The recommendation remained advice for the user to assess. This exchange does not demonstrate an automatic write to LiftTrack, Garmin or the calendar.
