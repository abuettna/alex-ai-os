CASE STUDY · WORKOUT REVIEW

Does this workout fit my week?

A Push workout was already planned in LiftTrack. I asked my AI coach to check it against my recent training and the rest of my plans.

LiftTrack · freddy · Google Calendar · Airtable

THE ANSWER IN THIS EXCHANGE

Keep the template.
Moderate the effort.

The coach recommended keeping the planned session while avoiding another progression test. No workout edit was needed or demonstrated.

WHAT WAS CHECKED

The template meets the rest of the week.

The visible activity messages report lookups across four connected sources. The response then discusses the template, previous strength sessions, recovery and upcoming training together.

WHAT CHANGED IN PRACTICE

The useful detail is in the execution.

The answer compared the template with previous performance and the surrounding training schedule. These are specific recommendations from that reply, not a record of a completed session.

01 / BENCH PRESS

18 kg. Controlled sets.

The reply references previous bench sets of 18 and 12 repetitions at 18 kg. Its recommendation for the planned session: 3 × 10–12 clean repetitions at 18 kg, rather than repeating a high-repetition effort just because it is possible.

The template load stays. The intended effort becomes more specific.

02 / INCLINE PRESS

14 kg as the starting point.

The previous session is described as including 14 kg × 16 and 16 kg × 13. The recommendation starts with 14 kg, with 16 kg only if the first set is very easy, without aiming to force a new record.

Previous performance informs the recommendation without making every session a progression test.

03 / THE OTHER EXERCISES

Keep the accessories familiar.

The response retains 4 kg lateral raises and 8 kg triceps extensions. It describes controlled repetitions for the remaining exercises and technically clean push-ups instead of a maximum-repetition test.

The exercise selection is retained; execution cues refine the plan.

04 / THE SURROUNDING WEEK

A strength session within a running plan.

The reply considers a recently completed long run, a planned day without running, a subsequent easy run and a later quality session. It assesses the Push workout as part of that sequence.

The calendar contributes context that the exercise template alone cannot provide.

“Keep the workout” can still come with a specific answer about weight, repetitions and effort.

HOW THE ANSWER BECAME SPECIFIC

Keep the plan. Adjust how it is executed.

The response went beyond confirming that a workout existed. This is an editorial summary of its reasoning, with personal details omitted:

  1. Check the schedule. Consider how the planned strength session fits around recent and upcoming endurance training.
  2. Compare the template with actual history. Distinguish the intended session from a fresh attempt to increase loads or repetitions.
  3. Use recovery and journal context. Bring the additional sources into the recommendation instead of judging the template alone.
  4. Return a practical answer. Retain the exercise template and recommend controlled effort rather than forcing progression.

The useful output was a decision about effort within an existing workout.
